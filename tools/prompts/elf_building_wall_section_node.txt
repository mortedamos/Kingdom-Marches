Game asset: Wall Segment, junction/watchtower bastion (race: Elf, category: wall).

A single game asset in a cel-shaded outline style: thick, clean black
outlines around the silhouette and major interior linework, sharp

tonal steps per surface -- base, shadow, occasional highlight -- no soft

zoomed out to a small size. Muted, desaturated, earthy fantasy palette
(olive greens, weathered browns, stone grays, faded ochres) -- no
saturated cartoon colors. Dark-fantasy mood, weathered and lived-in, not
cute or whimsical. Single consistent light direction from the upper left,
implied by the shadow/highlight color steps rather than rendered soft

This asset must stay legible when shrunk down to a very small size, so:
use strong contrast between neighboring color regions; keep ornamental
detail minimal -- favor a small number of bold, simple shapes over fine
texture that won't survive downscaling anyway.

Subject: a ROUND stone bastion/tower -- and ONLY a circle, see the
critical shape constraint below -- used wherever the wall bends, joins
another stretch of wall from any direction, or stands alone. A circular
structure built from the same roughly-stacked gray stone as the straight
wall segments, seen from directly above as a simple disc/circle shape.
This circle is sized so its own outer curved edge touches all FOUR sides
of the square frame at once -- the circle's diameter equals the full
width (and height) of the tile, so the curve itself is tangent to the
top, bottom, left, and right edges simultaneously.

CRITICAL shape constraint: the silhouette is a SINGLE CIRCLE and NOTHING
ELSE -- do NOT add any straight rectangular arms, stubs, spokes, or a
plus/cross shape reaching toward the corners or edges in addition to the
circle. Do not draw a smaller round platform sitting inside a separate

silhouette, shaped like a plain circle (or a circle with very slightly
rounded/scalloped stonework blocks along its rim, still reading as one

top/bottom/left/right edge midpoints) and nowhere else. The four corner
areas of the square frame, between the circle's curve and the frame's
actual corners, must be empty background -- no stone reaches into the
corners at all.

Because the bastion is round, a straight wall segment can connect into
it seamlessly from ANY of the four cardinal directions -- there is no
"front" or "back" side, the whole circular silhouette reads as a natural

ABSOLUTELY NOT an isometric or diagonal three-quarter diorama angle, no
